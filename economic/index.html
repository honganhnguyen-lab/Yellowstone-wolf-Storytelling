Lodging Leads the Way

The Lodging sector stands as the dominant economic engine among all sectors analyzed.

With $9.9 billion in economic output, Lodging generates nearly half of the economic activity across all sectors. It also leads in job creation, accounting for 36% of all employment in the analyzed sectors.

This demonstrates how travel and accommodation serve as foundational economic drivers, creating both direct spending and extensive employment opportunities.

Together, these two sectors account for one-third of all economic output and over 41% of jobs (103,000 combined jobs), highlighting how visitor-facing services drive both spending and employment.

The Restaurant sector creates more jobs per dollar of output than most other sectors, showing its role as an employment powerhouse in the economy.

The interconnected nature of these sectors shows how economic development requires a complete ecosystem of services supporting the major economic drivers.

The Lodging sector accounts for 45.2% of total labor income ($4 billion), closely matching its share of economic output.

However, labor income as a percentage of total output varies significantly between sectors, from 45% for Lodging down to 22% for Retail, reflecting differences in labor intensity and wage levels across the economy.

These variations highlight the complex relationship between economic activity, job creation, and income generation across different sectors.
